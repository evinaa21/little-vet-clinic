# Puff — app icon design brief

What ships today is a placeholder generated by `scripts/make_art.swift`: correct
palette, correct silhouette, not the finished thing. This is the brief for the
real icon.

---

## The one-line version

A soft pastel squircle, a small round mascot with a happy squint, and a mint
check badge. Cute, but with the weight and restraint of a system icon — not a
sticker.

## Palette

Straight from the app, so the icon and the widget read as one object.

| Role | Hex | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Lavender | `#E6D7F5` | primary background, bottom of the gradient |
| Blush pink | `#FFD9E8` | top of the background gradient |
| Mint | `#D3F5E3` | the check badge |
| Butter yellow | `#FFF3C4` | reserve — accent only, or leave out |
| Plum ink | `#5B4380` | facial features, never pure black |
| Mint deep | `#5FBF8E` | the checkmark stroke |
| Cheek pink | `#FF9EC4` at 55% | cheek blush |

Background is a soft vertical gradient, blush at the top → lavender at the
bottom, with a wide radial white glow (about 55% at the top, falling to nothing)
so it reads as glass rather than flat paint. No hard horizon line.

## Form

- Apple's rounded-rect grid: squircle at ~82% of the canvas, corner radius
  ~22.4% of the squircle's width, continuous curvature (not a plain rounded rect).
- Front-on, no perspective. Very soft ambient shadow under the mascot only —
  the squircle itself gets macOS's own shadow, don't bake one in.
- Must survive 16×16. The mascot's face and the badge are the only two shapes
  that need to read at that size; everything else can dissolve.

## Mascot

Pick **one** for the icon even though the app ships three. Recommendation: the
**blob** — it's the only one of the three that's distinctive at 16pt, and it
doesn't compete with the many capybara and cat icons already in the Mac world.

- A rounded, slightly-wider-than-tall white blob (about 92% white so it separates
  from the glow), corner radius ~46% of its height. Roughly 56% of the squircle's
  width.
- Face: two `^ ^` happy-squint arcs in plum ink, round line caps, stroke about
  2.2% of the canvas. Two cheek ellipses in cheek pink. No mouth — the squint
  carries it, and a mouth muddies at small sizes.
- The face sits slightly above centre so the badge has room.

If you go with the capybara instead: keep it blocky and calm — squarish head,
blunt muzzle, small round ears, fur `#C1936B` with `#A57A55` ears. The joke of a
capybara is its serenity; don't over-round it into a bear.

## Check badge

A mint circle at the bottom-right of the squircle, ~28% of the squircle's width,
overlapping the edge slightly so it feels like it's sitting on top. Inside: a
checkmark in mint deep, stroke ~13% of the badge diameter, round caps, drawn as
two segments with the elbow low-left of centre. Give the badge its own small,
tight shadow so it separates from the background.

## What to avoid

- Gradients with visible banding — these pastels band easily, dither if needed.
- Outlines around the squircle. Modern macOS icons have none.
- Drop shadows on the whole icon (macOS adds its own).
- Text, a literal checklist, or a clipboard. Every to-do app does the clipboard.
- Making the mascot too detailed to survive downscaling. Test at 16pt first, not last.

## Deliverables

PNG at 1024×1024, plus the full set the asset catalog expects:

```
icon_16x16.png      16     icon_16x16@2x.png     32
icon_32x32.png      32     icon_32x32@2x.png     64
icon_128x128.png   128     icon_128x128@2x.png  256
icon_256x256.png   256     icon_256x256@2x.png  512
icon_512x512.png   512     icon_512x512@2x.png 1024
```

Drop them into `Puff/Resources/Assets.xcassets/AppIcon.appiconset/`, replacing the
placeholders. The filenames in `Contents.json` already match, so nothing else
needs editing.

Alternatively, if the final art is vector or a single 1024 master, generate the
set with `sips`:

```bash
for s in 16 32 64 128 256 512 1024; do
  sips -z $s $s master.png --out "icon_${s}.png"
done
```

then rename to the table above.

## Menu bar icon

Separate problem, separate rules. It is currently the SF Symbol `cloud.fill` as a
template image, which is fine and adapts to light/dark automatically. If you want
a custom one: single colour, pure black on transparent, marked as a template
image, 18×18pt at 1x, and it must read at a glance in a crowded menu bar. The
mascot silhouette will not survive this — a simple cloud or a check-in-a-circle
will.
